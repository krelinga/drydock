A · idea

A dry dock is a basin you drain

You float a ship in, shut the gate, and pump the water out. The hull settles onto keel blocks and work that was impossible afloat becomes ordinary — you can walk underneath the thing and change it. The dock is not the ship. It does not sail. It is the prepared, isolated, drained place where the ship gets worked on.

Which is what Drydock is to a repo: a clone on host disk, a container brought up around it, an agent inside with credentials scoped to that one repo. The container survives nothing; the clone survives rebuild and delete. The dock persists, the vessel comes and goes.

So every candidate is a section through a drained basin with a container held clear of the floor on two keel blocks. The container does double duty on purpose: it is the vessel in the dock and it is the dev container. The gap underneath is the one detail that stops this reading as a generic box in brackets, so every candidate below is measured on whether it keeps that gap open.

What v1 got wrong, and what it was for

Draft v1 drew the basin as stepped walls — one altar step per side, every corner square. Rejected: too many right angles. Worth recording what the steps were doing, though, so the replacements get judged rather than just preferred for being new. The steps read as masonry: cut, built, load-bearing. They were the detail that made the enclosure say dock rather than bowl, bin, or basket.

Stepped

v1 · removed from icons/ superseded

Kept here only for comparison. Any curved basin gives up the masonry reading and leans harder on the keel blocks to carry the dock, since nothing else in the mark is specific to a dock.

B · basins

Same vessel, three ways to hold it

All three keep the container and keel blocks byte for byte and change only the basin path. None of them contains a right angle. Each is shown on both grounds at full size, then down the size ramp to the favicon.

Battered

drydock-mark-battered.svg flat floor · 109° join
light ground
dark ground

Reads as: excavated and built. Battered walls are what real dock walls do, so this is the closest thing to the stepped version with no square corner in it, and the only candidate that keeps a genuinely flat floor — which is what keel blocks stand on. Cost: it is still angular. If the objection to v1 was angles rather than right angles, this does not answer it.

Cradle

drydock-mark-cradle.svg one curve · vertical lips
light ground
dark ground

Reads as: held. One continuous curve, vertical at the lips so the arms stand up rather than splay, flat-tangent under the keel so the blocks have something to sit on. The softest of the three and the calmest at 16 px, because there is no corner anywhere for the rasteriser to blur. Cost: closest to reading as a plain bowl. The keel blocks are doing all the work of saying dock.

Flared

drydock-mark-flared.svg one curve · 8° open lips
light ground
dark ground

Reads as: open to receive — the lips lean 8° outward, which gives the mark motion the other two do not have and suits a dock whose gate is open. Cost: the flare spends the clear-space margin (its lip starts half a unit further in to pay for it), and splayed arms read slightly more like a vessel's own hull than like walls, which muddles what is holding what.

F · open

What is still yours to call

  1. Which basin. Battered keeps the built, excavated reading and a flat floor. Cradle is the calmest and the least likely to blur at 16 px. Flared has the most motion and the least certainty about what is holding what.
  2. What the vessel should be. v1 also offered the container with a >_ prompt on its face — the session rather than the workspace. Unresolved, and orthogonal to the basin; it gets redrawn on whichever basin wins rather than kept alive on a rejected one.
  3. Whether the door seam earns its place. It is the one interior detail, it is the first thing to vanish as the icon shrinks, and the mark is fine without it.
  4. Whether the accent should be the blue at all. It is the API colour in the architecture diagram, and the mark is currently spending it on the container. The violet is unused by the mark and already means “preview origin” elsewhere.

Once a basin and a payload are settled: redraw the badge and the one-colour currentColor form on the winning geometry (both existed in v1 and were dropped with the stepped basin, since both embedded it), cut the light/dark pair for Markdown <picture> embedding, and add the 180 px apple-touch PNG.
