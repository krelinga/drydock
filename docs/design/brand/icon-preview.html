A · brief

The basin is settled; the vessel is the open question

The battered basin won: straight walls battered inward to a flat floor, a 109° join, no right angle in it. It is fixed in every candidate below and no longer up for discussion. What changes is the vessel sitting in it, because the vessel is the only part of the mark with enough surface to carry two letters.

The ask is to work a DD into the mark to tie it to the name. That is a real constraint with a hard physical limit, so it is worth stating the limit before the options: at 16 px the whole mark is 16 pixels tall and the vessel is about five of them. No letterform survives that. So the question is not “can the icon say DD” — it is where the letters live, and what size they start reading at. Three places have the room:

— the vessel's silhouette, by making the vessel itself two D-shaped blocks (reads from about 24 px);
— the vessel's face, stencilled on a container that keeps its own shape (reads from about 48 px);
— the basin walls, which is the only place letters would survive to 16 px — and which does not work. See zone D.

B · candidates

Three ways to get the letters in

Twin — the vessels are the letters

drydock-mark-dd-twin.svg recommended
light ground
dark ground

Reads as: two vessels in the dock that happen to be D and D — and “more than one workspace in one dock” is true of the product, so the letters are earned rather than pasted on. Each letter is 5.55 × 8 units, a 0.69 width-to-height ratio, which is squarely in the range that reads as a D rather than a leaf or a pill. Each gets its own keel block. Cost: the vessel stops looking like a shipping container. Below 24 px the counters close and you get two accent blobs — legible as “something in the dock”, but no longer as letters.

Twin, outlined

drydock-mark-dd-twin-outline.svg same idea, lighter weight
light ground
dark ground

Reads as: letters first. Leaving the counters open keeps them reading as D's two sizes further down than the solid pair does, and the lighter weight stops the accent from dominating the ink. Cost: an outlined vessel is less obviously a solid object sitting in a dock — it reads a little like a diagram of the mark rather than the mark. Also the first to disappear on a low-contrast ground.

Stencil — letters on the container's face

drydock-mark-dd-face.svg keeps the container
light ground
dark ground

Reads as: a real container, which carries its owner's mark stencilled on the side — so this is the only option where the letters are doing something the subject actually does. The silhouette is untouched, so nothing is risked at small sizes. Cost: the letters are 3.5 × 5.2 units behind a 0.8-unit stroke. They are a header-size detail and nothing else: by 32 px they are a texture, by 20 px they are gone. If the point is for people to notice the DD, this is the weakest option.

D · rejected

Why the walls can't be the letters

Fig 1 — the walls given bowls at their lips. The batter has to be flattened to make room, which narrows the whole mark, and the right-hand letter comes out mirrored.

Letters in the basin walls are the only version that would survive to 16 px, because the walls are the silhouette. It was the first thing tried and it fails on two counts, both structural rather than matters of taste.

The proportions are wrong. A D reads as a D at a width-to-height ratio around 0.6–0.8. The wall is 16 units tall, and the space available for a bowl between the wall and the container is about 5 units wide, which gives 0.34 — a leaf, not a letter. Attaching the bowl to only the top half gets to 0.56 and leaves the wall continuing below it as a leg, which turns the D into something closer to a thorn. Flattening the batter to free more width makes it worse, not better.

One of them is always mirrored. A symmetric basin needs its two walls mirrored, and a mirrored D is not a D. Making both face the same way means an asymmetric dock with one wall shelved inward and the other bulging outward, which stops reading as a basin at all.

E · construction

What is fixed and what moved

basin
Settled: M3.1 8 8.6 24H23.4L28.9 8, stroke 2 units, round cap and join. Identical in all three candidates.
letter D
Stem, then a flat run, then a semicircular bowl whose radius is half the letter height — so the bowl is a true half-circle and the letter cannot go lopsided when it is resized. Solid versions carry a counter as a second subpath with fill-rule="evenodd".
twin, solid
Letters 5.55 × 8 at x 10 and 16.45, bowl radius 4, counter radius 2.2. Gap 0.9; 1.33 clear of the basin stroke.
twin, outlined
Same boxes once the 1.5-unit stroke is counted, which is why the path rect is 3.93 wide rather than 5.43 — the stroke has to be paid for out of the letter, or the two letters collide.
stencil
Letters 3.5 × 5.2 behind a 0.8-unit stroke, 1.1 clear of the container's edge, centred on the container's own centre at y 15.9.
keel blocks
Moved for the twin variants: one block centred under each letter rather than two under one container. x 11.8 and 18.2 solid, 11.7 and 18.3 outlined. The stencil keeps the original 12.6 and 17.4.
air gap
Unchanged at 2.1 units in all three: every vessel's underside still sits at y 20.9 and the floor stroke's top edge at y 23. That gap is the metaphor and nothing was allowed to spend it.
symmetry
Every letter pair and block pair sums to 32 across the mark, checked rather than eyeballed.
F · open

What is still yours to call

  1. Whether the DD is worth its cost at all. The baseline in zone A is still the cleanest mark of the four. The letters buy a tie to the name that only shows above 24 px, and the twin options buy it by giving up the container silhouette.
  2. If yes: twin or stencil. Twin puts the letters in the silhouette and makes them the subject. Stencil keeps the container and treats the letters as markings on it, which is what real containers do — at the price of being invisible below header size.
  3. A wordmark instead. The letters always read beside the mark, never inside it. A lockup — the battered mark plus “Drydock” set alongside — ties the name down completely and leaves the icon clean for the favicon. The UI header needs one regardless, so this is not a consolation prize.
  4. Whether the accent should be the blue. Still open from v2. It is the API colour in the architecture diagram, and the mark spends it on the vessel.

Once a vessel is settled: redraw the badge and the one-colour currentColor form on the winning geometry, cut the light/dark pair for Markdown <picture> embedding, and add the 180 px apple-touch PNG.
