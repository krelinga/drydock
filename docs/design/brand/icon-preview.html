A · the idea

A dry dock is a basin you drain

You float a ship in, shut the gate, and pump the water out. The hull settles onto keel blocks and work that was impossible afloat becomes ordinary — you can walk underneath the thing and change it. The dock is not the ship. It does not sail. It is the prepared, isolated, drained place where the ship gets worked on.

Which is what Drydock is to a repo: a clone on host disk, a container brought up around it, an agent inside with credentials scoped to that one repo. The container survives nothing; the clone survives rebuild and delete. The dock persists, the vessel comes and goes.

The container does double duty on purpose — it is the vessel in the dock and it is the dev container. The gap underneath is the one detail that stops this reading as a generic box in brackets, so it is fixed at 2.1 units and no revision was allowed to spend it. The DD is stencilled on the container's face because that is what real containers carry: the owner's mark, painted on the side.

For headers, buttons and empty states — anywhere the page ground is already known. Keeps the accent on the container, which the badge gives up. Watch the ramp: the stencilled letters are a header-size detail and are gone by 20 px. That was known and accepted when the stencil was chosen.

The badge

Brings its own ground, so it works where the surface belongs to someone else. Single knockout, which the contrast numbers forced rather than taste — see zone D. Below about 24 px the mark inside goes to a light blob and the badge is doing the identifying; that is the trade a badge exists to make.

One colour

Inherits currentColor, so it needs no palette of its own. The DD is deliberately absent: the letters are a stencil — they read by being knocked out of a filled container — and at one colour there is nothing for them to be knocked out of. An outlined container with same-colour letters inside it is mush by 24 px, which is the size this form exists to serve.

D · decisions

What was decided, and what the number was

Four questions, settled in order. Each alternative is in git history; the full reasoning is in brand-design.md. What follows is the evidence, not the narrative.

Where the letters could go. A D reads as a D at a width-to-height ratio of roughly 0.6–0.8; below that it reads as a leaf.

Letters in the basin walls were tried first and are the only version that would survive to 16 px, since the walls are the silhouette. Beyond the proportions, a symmetric basin needs mirrored walls — and a mirrored D is not a D.

The handsome dark navy badge is invisible on a dark page — 1.05 is the same colour. Steel is a deliberate choice against the brand-colour instinct: a dock is a grey structure, and a neutral badge leaves #1d4ed8 to go on meaning API traffic in the architecture diagrams rather than making a brand colour and a semantic colour the same colour. The known cost is that grey can read as not-yet-loaded; the dark-mode lift fights that.

The badge had to become a single knockout, and the number forced it. Keeping the accent container and knocking the letters out in the badge ground scores 1.00 on a blue badge — letters and ground are then literally the same colour — and 1.82 even with a lifted blue. So the container is the near-white and the letters are the ground. The consequence is that basin, blocks and container merge into one mass, and the air gap survives as three holes of ground colour under the vessel.

E · construction

The numbers that are easy to break

grid
0 0 32 32, 2 units of clear space on every side, everything on halves of a unit so a 16 px render lands on pixel edges.
basin
M3.1 8 8.6 24H23.4L28.9 8, stroke 2, round cap and join.
vessel
Container 10 × 10 at x 11, radius 1.6. Keel blocks 2 × 2.2 at x 12.6 and 17.4, overlapping the floor stroke by 0.1u so no hairline seam opens at fractional zoom.
air gap
2.1 units, constant. The metaphor lives here; nothing may spend it.
letters
Stem, flat run, then a bowl whose radius is exactly half the letter height — a true half-circle, so the letter cannot go lopsided when resized.
badge
rect 1 1 30 30 rx 7.5; mark scaled 0.78 about the centre, leaving 4.16u of padding (about 14%).
stroke comp
Every stroke inside the badge is divided by the scale — basin 2 → 2.56, stencil 0.8 → 1.03. Skipping this is the usual way a scaled-down mark comes out visibly thinner than its standalone version.
lip x
Set by the round cap, not the path: a cap reaches a full unit past its endpoint in every direction. That is why the lips sit at x 3.1 rather than 3.5.
pngs
Rasterised by render-icons.mjs from the same numbers, via signed distance fields — no rasteriser needed. The two are not generated from one source: change a path, change both.
F · open

What is left

  1. A wordmark. The letters always read beside the mark and never inside it — the stencilled DD is gone by 20 px. A lockup, the mark plus “Drydock” set alongside, is the only thing that makes the name unambiguous at any size, and the UI header needs one regardless. Separate asset, separate round.
  2. The >_ prompt payload. An early variant put a shell prompt on the container's face — the session rather than the workspace. It lost to the letters for the same surface. Worth revisiting only if the UI ever needs a second mark meaning “a session is live”.

Re-render this sheet after any geometry change — it is what caught every measured problem in the design note — then re-run render-icons.mjs so the PNGs do not drift from the SVGs.
