A · idea

A dry dock is a basin you drain

You float a ship in, shut the gate, and pump the water out. The hull settles onto keel blocks and work that was impossible afloat becomes ordinary — you can walk underneath the thing and change it. The dock is not the ship. It does not sail. It is the prepared, isolated, drained place where the ship gets worked on.

Which is what Drydock is to a repo: a clone on host disk, a container brought up around it, an agent inside with credentials scoped to that one repo. The container survives nothing; the clone survives rebuild and delete. The dock persists, the vessel comes and goes.

So every candidate is the same drawing — a section through a drained basin, two stepped walls and a floor, with a container held clear of the floor on two keel blocks. The container does double duty on purpose: it is the vessel in the dock and it is the dev container. The gap underneath is the one detail that stops this reading as a generic box in brackets, so nothing may crowd it.

Ruled out, and worth keeping ruled out: a whale or anything Docker-adjacent (Drydock treats Docker as a detail it never scrapes — borrowing its animal would claim a relationship the design refuses); an anchor, which holds a vessel in place at sea and is the opposite of a dry dock; a D monogram, which survives 16 px and says nothing; and a waterline, which read as “harbour” every time and destroys the only idea the mark carries.

B · candidates

The choice is what the vessel should be

Both line marks share the basin byte for byte. What differs is the payload: the workspace, drawn as a solid block, or the session, drawn as a container with a live shell on its face. Each is shown on both grounds at full size, then down the size ramp to the favicon.

One colour

Reads as: the same mark with the colour taken away. The container has to go hollow or it fills in as a blob, which costs the figure/ground contrast between vessel and basin. Use: buttons, disabled states, print, one-bit favicons — it inherits currentColor, so it needs no palette of its own.

D · construction

Everything lands on half a unit

viewBox
0 0 32 32, with 2 units of clear space on every side (dashed). Nothing but the mark enters that band.
basin
One path, shared by every variant: M3.5 8V15.5h5V24h15v-8.5h5V8
steps
One altar step per wall, 5 units of tread. Real docks have several; at 16 px a second step closes into a smudge.
stroke
2 units, round cap and join — exactly 1 px at a 16 px render.
blocks
2 × 2.2 at x 12.6 and 17.4, overlapping the floor stroke by 0.1u so no hairline seam opens up at fractional zoom.
vessel
Every variant's container holds the same 10 × 10 footprint, outer edges at x 11 and 21 — 1.5 units clear of each wall's inner face. Outlined variants shrink their path rect to 8.4u to keep that footprint once the stroke counts.
badge
Mark scaled 0.76 about centre, stroke thickened to 2.6 so the knockout weight matches the standalone mark.
theming
One file per variant. A <style> block sets the light palette on svg and overrides it under prefers-color-scheme: dark; every element also carries its light value as a presentation attribute, so a renderer that strips <style> still gets the light mark instead of a black silhouette.

F · open

What is still yours to call

  • Workspace or session. Solid block, or prompt on the container face. The 16 px column in zone B is the evidence; everything else is taste.
  • Whether the accent should be the blue at all. It is the API colour in the architecture diagram, and the mark is currently spending it on the container. The violet is unused by the mark and already means “preview origin” elsewhere.
  • Whether the door seam earns its place. It is the one interior detail in the default mark, it is the first thing to vanish, and the mark is fine without it. Keep it for the 96 px header, or drop it for one less thing to maintain.

Once a variant is picked: cut the light/dark SVG pair under icons/ following the diagram naming convention for Markdown <picture> embedding, add the 180 px apple-touch PNG out of the badge, and check the result beside Caddy's padlock in a real tab strip.
