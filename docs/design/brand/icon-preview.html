A · brief

One rule decides the ground, and it rules out the obvious answer

basinBattered · settled
vesselContainer · settled
lettersDD stencilled · settled
groundOpen — this sheet

A badge brings its own ground, which is the whole reason to have one: it stops depending on a page whose colour Drydock does not control. But the badge itself still sits on that page — and if its ground is close in value to the surface behind it, the rounded square dissolves and you are left with a floating mark and no badge at all.

So the ground has to hold its edge against four surfaces, not two: a white page, a light tab strip, a near-black page, and a dark tab strip. That is measurable, and measuring it kills the instinct most people have here — a dark, serious, navy badge. Drydock's own ink, #0f172a, scores 1.05 against a dark page. The badge is invisible: a 1.05 contrast ratio is the same colour. Slate-800 is 1.10. Graphite is 1.56, which is thin.

The grounds that work on both are mid-value and saturated — dark enough to hold white out of them, light enough to separate from a near-black page. Three are worth your time, each with a small luminance lift in dark mode so the dark side is not just “the light value, worse”.

B · grounds

Three that pass

Dock teal

drydock-badge-teal.svg #0f766e → #0d9488 recommended
on white
on a dark page

Why this one. Best numbers of the three by a clear margin — its worst edge across all four surfaces is 4.31, where blue manages 3.12 and steel 3.39. It also solves a problem the blue badge creates: in the architecture diagrams #1d4ed8 already means something — API traffic — and violet means preview origin. A blue badge makes the brand colour and a semantic colour the same colour. Teal keeps them separate and leaves the accent free to go on meaning what it means. Marine without being an anchor. Cost: a new hue in the palette, and teal is having a moment, so it will date faster than the blue.

Accent blue

on white
on a dark page

Why this one. It is already the system accent, so the icon and the documentation are visibly the same product with no new decisions and nothing new to maintain. Passes everything: worst edge 3.12, white knockout 4.94. Cost: the colour is doing two jobs, and it puts Drydock in the same blue as most developer tooling — findable, but not distinctive, in a row of favicons.

Steel

drydock-badge-steel.svg #475569 → #64748b the quiet one
on white
on a dark page

Why this one. Concrete, not branding — a dock is a grey structure, and this is the only ground that does not ask you to pick a brand hue at all. Worst edge 3.39, better than blue. Cost: it reads as disabled. A grey badge in a tab strip looks like a page that has not finished loading, and it gives up the one chance the icon has to be memorable.

D · detail

Corner radius, and the one two-tone that survives

rx 6 · 20%
Squarer. Reads as a tile or a chip; holds its shape best at 16 px.
rx 7.5 · 25%
The default here. Closest to what current platforms mask app icons to.
rx 9 · 30%
Softer, friendlier, and starts eating the mark's clear space at the corners.

Two-tone

A single knockout colour means the basin, the keel blocks and the container are all the same near-white, and since the blocks touch both the container and the floor they merge into one mass — the air gap survives as three holes of ground colour under the vessel. It works, but the dock and the vessel stop being separate things.

Dropping the basin to a mid tint and keeping the vessel at full knockout restores the hierarchy, and it is the only two-tone that survives the numbers. Every paler tint (blue-200, slate-300, teal-200) lands within 1.4 of white, which is too close to separate from the vessel. #93c5fd sits at 1.72 against the vessel and 3.0–4.2 against the grounds, which is enough for both.

E · construction

How the badge is built

badge
rect x=1 y=1 w=30 h=30 rx=7.5 — one unit of bleed inside the 32-unit box on every side, so the badge has room to be its own shape rather than running to the edge.
mark inside
Scaled 0.78 about the centre. The mark's ink is 27.8 × 18 units, so width is the binding constraint: 0.78 puts its edges at x 5.16 and 26.84, which is 4.16 units of padding inside the badge — about 14%, in line with how platforms pad app icons.
stroke compensation
Every stroke is divided by the scale so the knockout keeps the weight it has at full size: the basin's 2 units become 2.56, the stencil's 0.8 becomes 1.03. Skipping this is the usual way a scaled-down mark comes out visibly thinner than its standalone version.
knockout
#f8fafc, not pure white — a hair off white so it does not vibrate against a saturated ground.
why one colour
The measurement forced it. An accent container with the letters knocked out in the badge ground gives a contrast of 1.00 on the blue badge, because the letters and the ground are then literally the same colour, and 1.82 even with a lifted blue. The container has to be the near-white and the letters the ground.
theming
Each badge file ships the light ground as a presentation attribute and lifts it under prefers-color-scheme: dark. The knockout does not change.
F · open

What is still yours to call

  1. Which ground. Teal on the numbers and on keeping the accent free; blue if you would rather not introduce a hue; steel if the mark should not be branded at all.
  2. Flat or two-tone. Flat is one colour and no decisions. Two-tone with #93c5fd on the basin restores the dock-versus-vessel hierarchy the single knockout flattens.
  3. Corner radius. 25% unless you want the squarer tile. Worth knowing that iOS masks the apple-touch icon to its own shape regardless, so this radius only governs the favicon and anywhere the SVG is used directly.
  4. A wordmark. Still open from v3, and still the only thing that makes the name unambiguous at any size. The UI header needs one regardless.

Once a ground is picked: redraw the one-colour currentColor form, cut the light/dark pair for Markdown <picture> embedding, and export the 180 px apple-touch PNG from the badge.
